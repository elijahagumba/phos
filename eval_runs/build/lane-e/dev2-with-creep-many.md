## The class I built to test lane 2 is caught by the engine that cannot see breadth

`creep_many` — three or four instruments each reporting 3% to 8% high at once — is caught **10 of
10 by the frozen engine**, on its ordinary per-signal columns. Lane 2's breadth column adds
nothing, and lane 2 ties again at 32 of 70.

ADR 0285 fixed the reading in advance: "If the class turns out to be trivially caught, or never
caught, that is the result." It is trivially caught, and the reason is instructive rather than a
mistake in the magnitudes. The frozen engine's loop score predicts each signal from the **others**.
Biasing a reading by 5% while the rest of the plant carries on normally breaks exactly the
relationship that score measures, so the departure is visible in that one signal alone and no
breadth is needed to see it.

**So lane 2 needs something harder than a multi-signal attack: it needs an attack invisible
per-signal.** That means a manipulation consistent with the plant's own correlations — biasing
level and flow *together*, in the ratio the plant normally holds, so each reading stays plausible
given the others and only the joint pattern is wrong. That is an attacker who knows the plant
model, which is a materially more sophisticated adversary and a materially harder generator
problem than the one ADR 0285 solved.

Lane 2 therefore stays undeclared, with a sharper reason than before: not "the corpus lacks a
multi-signal attack" but "a multi-signal attack a cross-signal detector can see does not need
breadth". Writing that attacker is the next real question for this lane, and it is a declaration
of its own, not an adjustment to ADR 0285.

## Lane 1 holds up on a second corpus, and improves precision

53 of 70 against 32, on runs generated by a changed generator with a class lane 1 has never seen.
`recon` 0 to 10, `replay` 5 to 10, `rogue_command` 4 to 10, repeating the held-out pattern of
ADR 0276 on independent data.

**And benign alerts went down, 5 of 20 to 3 of 20.** That is not a new detector being quieter — it
is the fixed budget working as intended. Eight alerts a shift are allocated to the strongest
events, so strong attribution evidence displaces weak signal noise. A gain in catches bought a
gain in precision rather than costing one, which is the opposite of the usual trade and worth
saying.

`mimic_maintenance` is 2 of 10 for all three engines, caught by the signal columns, consistent
with it being a provenance attack whose evidence lives in the work-order inlet (A5, ADR 0069).
