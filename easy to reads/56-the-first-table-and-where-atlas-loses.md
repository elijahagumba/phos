# 56 — The first table, and where Atlas loses

The starting line was one table. Atlas, and the three simple detectors that had already
been checked against their authors' own program, on the same files, with the same budget:
eight alerts in an eight-hour shift. The question is how many of the labelled attacks
still contain one of the alerts that were kept.

Atlas is the ordinary ungated distance model, with the threshold already used in public
runs. Nothing was tightened after the numbers came back.

On the two HAI editions that are actually on disk, Atlas catches fewer attacks inside
that budget than the simplest opponent. HAI 20.07 is 9 of 38 for Atlas and 15 of 38 for
the range detector. HAI 21.03 is 7 of 50 and 14 of 50. Two later HAI editions are only
pointers in the download, so they are rows in the table and they are not scores. A
missing file counts as not won.

On the water-network capture, Atlas catches all five attacks and the range detector
catches three. That is the one judging corpus where Atlas is ahead.

A second score, the one that paints a whole attack as caught if any moment inside it
was, makes Atlas look stronger on HAI 20.07. That score is in the table. It is not the
headline. The contest's timing score is beside the headline too, and on the water
network one of the simple detectors beats Atlas on that score even while losing the
budget.

The distillation column and the short grid are printed and they do not decide the
comparison. One is a process upset made by the plant's own operators. The other is a
phase label on a recording too short to be a control-room shift.

The frozen public numbers were not replaced. One command writes the table again.

---

**The short version.** At eight alerts a shift, Atlas loses to a range check on both
HAI editions we could score, and wins on the water network. Two HAI editions are not
scored. Nothing was adjusted afterwards.
