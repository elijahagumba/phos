# 43 — A study without operators is not a field result

**CRUDE_NON_OPERATOR_NOT_A_FIELD_STUDY.** Not a field study. Not operational usefulness.

U4 gave us a page a person can act on without reading a probability. U5 asks the next question:
does that page actually help? Same incidents, three ways — raw numbers, a threshold buzzer, and
Atlas's plain-English brief — and then: do people reach the right call faster, and do they raise
fewer false alarms?

We cannot sit that study yet. The people who can read a plant are gated on a partner conversation
(U7), and U7 is not started. What this item built is the *instrument*: scoring, gold labels that
follow the evidence rather than Atlas, a Latin square for when humans arrive, and a crude first
run that is labelled on every page it prints.

The crude run has **zero** human subjects. A mechanical policy that cannot read process data
walks the three arms so we know the machinery computes. On the Atlas arm it copies the U4
assessment. If that arm then looks "better," that is the policy, not a person, and it is
forbidden to quote as usefulness.

Sentiel still cannot name a behaviour fact that would settle its competing explanations — the
templates declare no contradictions — so that case sits outside the primary table rather than
being assumed away.

What would make this a real study is plant-literate people, recruited through the partner
conversation, sitting each incident once. Until then PHOS has an executable protocol and no
operational-usefulness measurement.
