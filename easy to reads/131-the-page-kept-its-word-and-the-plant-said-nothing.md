# 131. The page kept its word, and the plant said nothing

*All eight counts came back zero. The plant also broke none of its own laws,
so the two counts that mattered most were never tested on it. ADR 0336.*

The run happened. All eight counts came back zero, which is what was written
down beforehand, so the bar is met.

The honest version takes a second sentence.

The simulated plant broke none of the laws it had named. Thirteen runs, and
every one of the thirty-nine pages built from its rows came back empty. Not
one false law in any of them.

That matters because of where the sentences came from. The report shows
thirteen pages that are completely full, eight lines each, and thirteen runs
with more breaks than there are slots. Read quickly, that looks like the
plant filling an operator's shift. It is not the plant. The harness builds a
made-up page once per run, twelve invented breaks a second apart, on purpose,
to check that the page keeps only the earliest eight and drops the rest. It
has to invent them, because ordinary rows need not produce a ninth break, and
the record said so in advance.

So all one hundred and four lines the counts judged came off that made-up
page. Thirty-nine empty pages produce no lines at all. Which means the check
that every line names its law, and the check that no line carries one of the
five refused words, were both measured entirely on invented input. The word
check is the one that had just been fixed, and putting it under test is why
the bar was written a second time. The plant never gave it a sentence to
judge.

One count did get real exercise. Every one of the thirty-nine real reports
sorted all six laws into exactly one of held, broken, and unstated, with no
law in two lists and none missing. Two shapes came back: three held and three
unstated where the plant's history had rows, and six unstated where the page
was handed the controller reading alone and had nothing to check against.
Both are correct answers.

The record had a fallback ready for the opposite outcome. If ordinary running
filled all eight slots, the page would be unreadable on this plant and the
checks would stay exactly as written, because the answer to a full page is
not a bigger page. That fallback did not fire. The queue was not shown to be
unreadable. It was shown to be empty, which is a weaker sentence and the true
one.

An empty page is not a safe plant. A break is a false law, not an attack. No
count here is a catch rate or a false-alarm rate, and none of them may be
read as one. This is a simulation.

What the run does show is that the page keeps its promises about shape: the
ordering and the dropping were tested at twelve breaks, which is the case
that matters, and they held.

Nothing was retuned. Version 1 stays at eight of twelve.
