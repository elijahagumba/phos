# 154. The first sentence was not true

*The public front page opened on a claim a reader could falsify in one search. It is corrected.
The page gains diagrams, mathematics and charts, every plotted number read from the record at
build time, and one chart is refused because the record does not hold it. These notes publish
too, and one is held back. ADR 0381.*

The public front page opened with "PHOS is a behavioural cybersecurity company." There is no
company. There is one researcher, working with AI coding agents, and the page's own footer
already said so. The first sentence was the one a reader met first, and it was the overclaim the
whole project argues against. So it is corrected, under the rule that already allowed correcting
something untrue.

The page also gains pictures and mathematics. That needed a separate decision, because it
corrects no untruth: a freeze on design work stands until week 13, and this is week 4. The
decision was taken and recorded, and it admits three things only. Diagrams are written as text a
reader can read in the change itself. The page's contract is written as mathematics beside the
code that enforces it, with a plain sentence under each line. Charts are drawn in the house
style, each carrying its decision number and its caveat on the image. No logo, no colour, no new
typeface, no film.

Every number a chart shows is read from the committed record when the chart is built, and a test
fails when a plotted number stops matching its source. A chart cannot drift from the record it
cites.

A fourth chart was asked for and refused. The question was how loud each alert was, against
whether it was an attack. The record keeps where the six true alerts sat in the queue and the
scores at seven budget cuts. It does not keep a loudness paired with an attack flag for every
alert, and the corpus that could rebuild them may not be read again. So the chart is not drawn,
and the page says why, rather than drawing a different chart under the same heading.

These plain-English notes now publish with the page. One is held back: note 132 walks through a
commercial record, and commercial material is not published. The index keeps its number and
declines it, exactly as the record itself is handled. The decision index also explains its own
gaps now: a missing number is reserved and never written, held on a branch that has not merged,
or withheld. A missing number is not a missing record.

Nothing was scored. Version 1 still stands at eight of twelve.
